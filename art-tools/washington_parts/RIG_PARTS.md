# Washington: the rigging-parts test (IK arms)

The owner's test of drawing a character AS PARTS for the IK arm
(`web/puppet/ik.ts`), beside Lincoln's IK prototype, which was cut from his
existing pose drawings. Washington sits at the same table, so both can be
compared on one screen with `?ik=1`. His rest pose already has the hands apart.

Attach `sources/poses_a.png` (his rest pose is top left) and
`sources/washington_sheet.png`, in that order.

```
Rigging parts sheet for 2D cut-out animation: George Washington's ARMS AND HANDS, exactly as in the FIRST attached image's top-left pose (seated at a poker table, three-quarter view facing the viewer's right, forearms along the table's edge, hands resting apart) and the SECOND attached character sheet: the same dark blue Continental Army coat sleeves with buff cuffs, the same white lace at the wrists, the same skin colour, the same line weight and flat cartoon style. Every piece drawn at EXACTLY the size and angle it has in that top-left pose, so it can be laid back over it.

Pure white background (#FFFFFF). Every piece separate, with a closed dark outline and a wide gap all round it; nothing touches or overlaps. No text, no labels, no body, no head, no table.

For his RIGHT arm (the one nearer the viewer):
- The upper arm: the coat sleeve from the shoulder down to the elbow, ending in a ROUNDED cap of sleeve that runs a little PAST the elbow (so the forearm can turn under it without a gap)
- The forearm: the coat sleeve from the elbow to the wrist with its buff cuff and the white lace, its elbow end ROUNDED and drawn a little longer than needed (it hides under the upper arm), its wrist end open: NO hand
- Hands, each on its own, each with a short stub of wrist that runs INTO the cuff (so it can turn inside it without a gap), each at the angle it would have at the end of that forearm:
  1. resting flat on the felt, relaxed, fingers together (exactly as in the top-left pose)
  2. the index finger pointing down to tap the table, the other fingers curled
  3. cupped over two playing cards, thumb lifting the near corner
  4. pushing a short stack of chips forward
  5. laying two cards down face down, squared
  6. turning two cards face up (BLANK white card faces)
  7. open, palm up, a little raised (explaining)
  8. thumb and fingers pinching, as if straightening the other wrist's cuff

For his LEFT arm (the one further away), the same upper arm and forearm, and these hands:
  1. resting flat on the felt, relaxed (exactly as in the top-left pose)
  2. lifted a little, palm down, wrist turned so the right hand can straighten its cuff
  3. fingers drumming once on the felt

Keep the simple, chunky, flat cartoon style of the attached images: few lines, big smooth shapes, flat colour, no fine detail or texture.
```
