# The Recording Angel: every pose, and the prompts

The dealer at the Last Crossing (the owner's call). Death plays the finale, so
he does not deal it. The Recording Angel does: the one whose work is to
witness and write down, come down to the dock house because this hand has to
be witnessed. Nobody would doubt the honesty of the deal, and it hints at the
sequel (other halls have an interest in a mortal among legends) without
spending any of it: the Angel is never named, never explains, and says almost
nothing. Death's dry line: "Someone has to witness it."

The finale is laid out once, differently: the Stranger (the player) in the
left chair, Death in the right, the Angel between them at the back, front-on,
where Death deals everywhere else. The ferryman's lantern stays out on the
river, as the room has always had it.

**A working angel, not a glorious one:** ageless, calm and kind, faintly
amused; a plain white robe with a thin gold edge, sleeves pushed up like a
clerk at work, a quill tucked behind one ear and ink on the fingertips, large
off-white wings folded close behind, their feathers dusty grey at the tips,
and a thin plain gold halo. NOT radiant or glowing (the finale is lit by one
lamp), no sword, no trumpet, no armour, not a cherub, not a statue, nothing
creepy. Nothing about it says man or woman.

Drawn: `sources/angel_sheet.png` (the owner: "I do like the angel"), a
young-looking man with curly hair; the prompts below follow the drawing.

Earlier draws, kept as a record (`sources/rejected/`): a ferryman in an ochre
oilskin (a fisherman, not Death's partner) and a ferryman in Death's colours
("unreasonably creepy"; the owner).

Built like Death's dealer kit (`art-tools/death_parts/POSES.md`): front-on,
never mirrored, so the prompts say "the hand on the LEFT OF THE PICTURE";
complete pose drawings, a master head with face layers. **No tells**: it is
the house. Heads up, it deals to two chairs: the left of the picture (the
Stranger) and the right (Death).

## Prompts

One fresh ChatGPT chat for all five. The first prompt attaches TWO images, in
this order: Death's dealer character sheet
(`art-tools/death_parts/sources/death_sheet.png`) for the layout and the
style, then Death's dealer pose sheet A
(`art-tools/death_parts/sources/poses_a.png`) for how a front-on dealer sits.
From the second prompt on, attach the Angel's own character sheet and,
once it exists, its pose sheet A, and NOTHING of Death's (the White House
lesson: an attached sheet leaks its character).

### Character sheet

```
Character sheet for 2D animation rigging: the Recording Angel, whose work is to witness and write things down, come to a dock house at night to deal cards at the last table of a long game. Copy ONLY the LAYOUT and the STYLE of the FIRST attached sheet (what goes where, and how it is drawn): take NOTHING of its character. No hood, no skull, no black robe, no bone hands, no brooch. The SECOND attached image shows how a dealer sits front-on behind the table: copy that posture only.

The Recording Angel: ageless and calm, a kind, attentive face, faintly amused; short, neat hair; nothing about the Angel says man or woman. A plain white robe with a thin gold edge at the neck and sleeves, the sleeves pushed up to the forearms like a clerk at work; a quill pen tucked behind one ear; a little ink on the fingertips; large off-white wings folded close behind the shoulders, the feathers dusty grey at the tips, their tops no higher than the top of the head; a thin, plain gold halo just behind the head. A working angel, not a glorious one: NOT radiant, NOT glowing, no light coming from the Angel at all (it is lit only by the room's lamp); no sword, no trumpet, no armour, NOT a cherub, NOT a statue, NOT creepy, NOT solemn.

Pure white background (#FFFFFF). Every piece separate, with a closed dark outline and a wide gap all round it; nothing touches or overlaps. No text, no labels, no frames, no cast shadows.

The angle: FRONT-ON, facing the viewer, like the dealer in the SECOND attached image: the Angel deals from the far side of the table, facing the player.

Draw, all front-on, at the same size:
- Finished heads with the halo, the quill and the robe's collar: level, eyes open, a calm faint smile; eyes closed (a blink); looking to the left of the picture; looking to the right of the picture; looking down at the table
- The torso in the robe, with the folded wings behind it: no head, cut off straight below the waist, the upper arms down to the elbows; NO forearms, NO hands
- Two forearms with the pushed-up sleeves and the inky fingertips, lying on a table, open at the elbow end
- A round portrait for a nameplate: the head and shoulders inside a circle, facing the viewer
- Four seated reference poses, waist up behind the far curved edge of a round green poker table, front-on: at rest, forearms on the table, hands apart, one hand resting on a squared deck of cards; dealing a card; shuffling the deck; taking the quill from behind the ear

Cards have plain dark backs; any card face shown is BLANK white.

STYLE, the most important rule: match the FIRST attached image (the game's dealer sheet) exactly: the same colours of light, outlines, line weight and painted cartoon shading, so this figure sits beside that one in the same room. Big clear readable shapes and a silhouette that reads at a tiny size; grown-up proportions, never chibi, never cute. NOT photoreal, NOT a 3D render. No scythe, no hourglass, no book, no scroll, no candles, no chair.
```
