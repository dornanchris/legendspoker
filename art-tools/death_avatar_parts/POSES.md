# Death, as a player (online avatar): every pose, and the prompts

Death as a PLAYER: the avatar the design doc promises for beating the game ("Beating the game unlocks Death as a playable avatar"), for ONLINE play, which is not designed yet: drawn now so the art exists if online happens (the owner). The dealer puppet is front-on and always sits dead centre; an avatar sits in any chair, so this one is drawn three-quarter, facing the viewer's right and mirrored as the seat needs, in the dealer's own style so he is plainly the same figure.

**Non-negotiable #3 governs everything it does.** In multiplayer an avatar's movements must have ZERO correlation with the player's cards, so it has NO tells. Sheet A is the poker actions and sheet B the public beats (all in, showing, a pot won or lost, heads up, talking): they show what everyone at the table already saw. Sheet C is a COSMETIC loop played at random, tagged LOOP, deliberately unlike any single-player character's tell or idle, so nobody tries to read it. Its hands are empty in every pose: the game moves the cards and chips.

## Prompts

One fresh ChatGPT chat for all five. The first prompt attaches TWO images, in this order: Lancelot's character sheet (`art-tools/lancelot_parts/sources/lancelot_sheet.png`) for the three-quarter layout, then Death's own character sheet (`art-tools/death_parts/sources/death_sheet.png`) for his look and style. ### Character sheet

```
Character sheet for 2D animation rigging: Death, the dealer of the attached second image, drawn now as a PLAYER sitting at the table, in a three-quarter view. Copy ONLY the LAYOUT of the FIRST attached sheet (what goes where): take NOTHING of its character. Take his LOOK and STYLE from the SECOND attached image, exactly.

Death, exactly as on the SECOND attached image: tall and narrow in a black hooded robe with a deep purple lining, ragged at the hems, crossed with worn leather straps, a gold skull brooch on a short chain at the collarbone; under the hood, a plain skull in shadow, with NO glowing eyes and NO leer; long bone hands. Tired, courteous and patient: a host who has seen every hand there is. He holds NO deck and NO cards: here he is a player, not the dealer. No scythe, no hourglass.

Pure white background (#FFFFFF). Every piece separate, with a closed dark outline and a wide gap all round it; nothing touches or overlaps. No text, no labels, no frames, no cast shadows.

The angle: a gentle three-quarter view, turned about 25 degrees from front-on, facing the viewer's right (NOT front-on like the dealer sheet). The skull in the hood and both shoulders clearly visible. NOT profile.

Draw, all facing the viewer's right, at the same size:
- Finished heads, each the hood with the skull in its shadow, with the collar and the brooch: level, jaw closed; talking, the jaw dropped a little; tilted down toward the table; turned toward the viewer
- The torso in the robe, straps and brooch: no head, cut off straight below the waist, the upper arms down to the elbows; NO forearms, NO hands
- Two forearms in the robe's sleeves with long bone hands, lying on a table, pointing toward its centre, open at the elbow end
- A round portrait for a nameplate: the hooded head and shoulders inside a circle, facing the viewer's right
- Four seated reference poses, waist up behind the curved edge of a round green poker table, forearms resting on its edge, bone hands apart: at rest; looking down at the felt; fingers steepled in front of the hood; leaning back, weary

STYLE, the most important rule: match the SECOND attached image (Death's own sheet, as the game's dealer) exactly: the same character, colours, outlines, line weight and shading, so this player and the dealer are plainly the same figure. Big clear readable shapes and a silhouette that reads at a tiny size. NOT photoreal, NOT a 3D render, NOT gory, NOT a horror monster: a tired, courteous figure.
```
